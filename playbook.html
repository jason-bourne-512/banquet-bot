Captain Playbook
A full shift, start to finish — tap a step to open
The shift, in order
📻
Arrival — first things
▶
  • Walkie charged and on you — before anything else.
  • Get the keys and open the ballrooms and boardrooms (sometimes the day before, sometimes day-of).
  • Gather every BEO for the day. Confirm they’re corrected and current; print fresh copies if needed.
  • Put up directional signage pointing guests to their meeting.
  • Set door signs for each event.
🍳
Room and kitchen sync
▶
  • Walk every room — general read on setup and temperature.
  • Sync with the kitchen: timing for every event today, every meal on each BEO, and old or outdated event orders cleared from their side.
  • Help staff set up buffets, beverages and breaks as needed.
🗣️
Staff briefing
▶
  • Arrive a little before your service staff.
  • Give them a rundown of the BEO.
  • Go over closing duties right when they arrive — not left for the end of the night.
  • Answer their questions before doors open.
⏱️
Final check — 15 minutes out
▶
  • All staff on the floor 15 minutes before the event starts.
  • Buffets and drinks ready — double-check every item is actually there.
  • Once food is ready, confirm staff are prepping that event’s later breaks or meals.
🤝
During service
▶
  • Talk to the client contact when they arrive; ask if they need anything.
  • Greet guests as they come in.
  • Help clear or service the buffet.
  • Stay in touch with the client contact for timing or anything they need.
  • After lunch: make sure breaks go out on time.
🧺
Building for tomorrow — all day
▶

Not one step at the end of the night. It happens in pieces whenever there’s a gap.

  • Servers pull linen from housekeeping.
  • Get silverware from dish; roll it clean and organized into the stock.
  • Build beverages for tomorrow’s events.
  • Build buffets for tomorrow’s events.
🧾
End of day — admin and reporting
▶
  • Write the Banquet Captain Report.
  • Post checks and the recap in Excel, and in Lightspeed.
  • Close out the checks in CI/TY.
  • Email the recap to the managers so front desk can run the night audit.
  • Post tomorrow’s event orders on the wall so staff can get ready — with server names assigned.
  • Set up rooms for the next day.
  • Keep tomorrow’s contacts warm in case anything changes.
  • Resolve anything unclear in tomorrow’s BEOs before it hits the floor.
Reading a BEO
🔎
The beverage isn’t always its own line
▶

When a buffet includes a beverage, it’s usually the last item listed — e.g. a lunch buffet ending in “Iced Tea, Freshly Brewed Little City Coffee and Assorted Herbal Teas.”

Don’t decide a group has “no beverage” until you’ve checked the last line of the buffet. Only groups with a separate All-Day Beverage or Continuous Break line have it broken out.
