Safety & Conduct
⚠️
Banquet Area Safety
▶
  • Do not run.
  • Plan ahead — rushing leads to slips, falls, and injuries.
  • Don't ignore spills or problems. Discard broken glass properly, respect knives.
  • Never overload a serving tray — risk of wrist/shoulder injury and spilling hot food on guests.
  • Clean up spills immediately. If you can't, stand by the spill until help arrives.
  • Pour coffee and hot liquids away from people.
  • Keep your station neat — check lights, chairs, and flooring for hazards.
  • Know the location of every fire extinguisher.
  • Know emergency procedures for fire and weather.
  • Know how to help a choking victim (CPR Poster).
  • Report any suspicious activity to a manager.
  • Use extreme caution when entering and exiting doors.
🧼
Hygiene & Unsanitary Habits
▶

These habits must be eliminated — they are universally unsafe even when common.

  • Never place fingers on top of a plate — always serve from the bottom.
  • Always carry glasses on trays, never in your hand.
  • Hold utensils at the bottom (handle end), not by the business end.
  • Don't stack glasses when clearing.
  • Always use utensils when serving food — never bare hands.
  • Always wash hands properly before serving.
  • Always arrive in clean, ironed uniform with groomed appearance.
🤲
Proper Hand Washing (WHO Method)
▶

Duration: 40–60 seconds total.

  1. Wet hands with water.
  2. Apply enough soap to cover all hand surfaces.
  3. Rub hands palm to palm.
  4. Right palm over left dorsum with interlaced fingers, and vice versa.
  5. Palm to palm with fingers interlaced.
  6. Backs of fingers to opposing palms with fingers interlocked.
  7. Rotational rubbing of left thumb clasped in right palm, and vice versa.
  8. Rotational rubbing, backwards and forwards with clasped fingers of right hand in left palm, and vice versa.
  9. Rinse hands with water.
  10. Dry hands thoroughly with a single-use towel.
  11. Use towel to turn off faucet. Your hands are now safe.

Service Standards
🍽️
Order of Service
▶

These rules apply to all plated meal events. Each server starts the same course at the same Guest at each table.

Key Rules
  • LEFT Food is served from guest's left with left hand.
  • RIGHT All beverages served from guest's right with right hand.
  • RIGHT All food/beverage (except bread & butter plates) cleared from guest's right.
  • Walk clockwise around the table when serving.
  • Service starts at the 12:00 chair.
  • Ladies are served first, then remaining guests.
  • Salad dressings cleared from left side before entrée.
Sequence
  • Greet guests at door; assist with seating and napkins.
  • Tray stands brought in after guests are seated.
  • Front & back server pick up first course; front server serves while back server runs more food.
  • Bread & butter plates and knives cleared from left before dessert.
  • Cream and sugar served; coffee cup and saucer from guest's right.
  • Dessert served from guest's left; cleared from right.
  • After-dinner mints passed or placed on table. Thank guests as they leave.
🌅
Breakfast Standards
▶
Buffet
  • Toasters recommended where bagels or toast are served.
  • Juices offered at buffet table AND tableside by servers.
Plated
  • Juices offered tableside upon seating.
  • Breakfast breads pre-set before doors open. Two baskets per table. Pre-set butter, margarine, preserves, cream, and sugar.
Coffee Service
  • Pre-set all coffee cups and saucers. Offer and pour as guests are seated.
  • Regular coffee pots may be pre-set for refills. Decaf and tea available on request.
  • Always have coffee and juices available throughout the meal. Juices always poured tableside.
Tea Service
  • Present tea box with 8 selections or china plate with 4. Hot water in tea pots.
  • Do not steep tea — let the guest do it unless they request otherwise.
🍱
Plated Lunch & Dinner Standards
▶
Bread Service
  • Bread is always the FIRST course — unless soup is en croute.
  • Two bread baskets pre-set; minimum 3 varieties; 1.5 pieces per guest.
  • Bread may be warm — never held in a hot box. A room-temp roll beats a poorly held warm roll.
  • Butter and margarine must be pre-set.
Key Rules
  • Beverages offered as soon as guests are seated.
  • Appetizers are a course — not pre-set unless on the BEO.
  • No saltine crackers with soup. Use specialty crackers that match.
  • Always check BEO for vegetarian or alternative meal requests.
  • Service and clearing of each course on signal from the Captain.
  • If a program follows dinner, assign a server to remain. Remove all trays and stands before the program begins.
Dinner — After Entrée
  • Clear entrée, bread & butter plate, S&P. Move dessert fork and coffee spoon into place setting.
  • Deliver cream and sugar → place cup and saucer as one piece → pour coffee → serve dessert.
☕
Coffee Break / Continental Standards
▶

Set up 15 min before contracted start time. Max coffee holding time: 90 minutes.

  • Mineral waters served chilled.
  • Ice in spotlessly clean bucket with clean plastic or metal scoop.
  • Coffee urns must be cleaned and polished — daily rinsing is not enough.
  • Standard: coffee mugs. VIP events: cups with saucers, stacked no more than 3 high.
  • Cream standard: half & half AND skim milk, clearly labeled in thermal servers.
  • Place a torn sugar packet in the trash container to indicate its purpose.
  • Provide a location for guests to deposit dirty dishes — clear it frequently.
  • Never add fresh coffee to old coffee. Always empty, rinse, refill.
  • Breaks for 50+ guests must be double-sided or have multiple setups.
  • Fruit preserves, jams, and honey in individual servings only.
  • When attending a room during a break, restock Clutter-Free business setting and tidy trash.
🍴
Table Setup: Napkins, Flatware & Glassware
▶
Napkins
  • Never place in a glass or cup.
  • Place on base plate, between forks and knives, or across top of place setting.
  • If a guest gets up mid-service, refold napkin and tidy the place setting.
Center of Table
  • All settings oriented to the north wall and the 12:00 chair.
  • Salt on right, pepper on left (as guest faces it). Cream behind salt; sugar behind pepper.
  • Bread baskets pre-set at 1:00 and 7:00.
  • Butter and margarine dishes at 3:00 and 9:00. Two dishes per table.
Flatware
  • All flatware cleaned, polished, and wiped before setting. Handle by handles only.
  • Set ½ inch from edge of table, straight. Square alignment per place setting.
  • Max 3 pieces per side. If more needed, bring additional flatware with the relevant course.
  • Dessert fork at top, handle left. Coffee spoon above it, handle right.
  • If a utensil is dropped, replace it BEFORE picking it up.
Glassware
  • Handle only by base or stem. Inspect for chips and cracks.
  • Water glass above tip of inside knife. Wine glasses at 45° angle below water glass.
  • Max 4 glasses per setting (including water). Never lift glassware when pouring.
🗂️
Clearing Tray Standards
▶

Goal: minimize noise and keep trays tidy. Never scrape food onto the tray or stack dishes in front of guests.

  • No more than 3 place settings cleared at a time.
  • Place similar flatware together to help the dish room.
  • Food cleared onto a plate in the CENTER of the tray — never directly onto the tray.
  • Don't overload trays. Lift with knees, not your back.
  • Coffee pots, water pitchers, and wine bottles should never be on trays.
  • China and glassware must be cleared to separate trays.
  • Plate covers never used during clearing.
Clearing B&B Plates

Stand between and slightly behind two guests. Clear the left guest's entrée from the right with your right hand → transfer to left hand → clear that guest's B&B plate from the left with your left hand → transfer to entrée plate in your right hand.

🪜
Tray Stands
▶
  • Tray stands go into the room AFTER guests are seated.
  • Bring 2–3 stands and 2–4 trays when you first set up.
Supply StandTrays upside down on the stand, top one right-side up to hold wine, water pitchers, etc.
Landing StandAlways kept clear so an available landing tray is ready. No top tray stacked over an upside-down one.

Always bend at the knees when lifting — never the back.

😊
Friendly Service & Attentiveness
▶
  • Greet entering guests: "Good morning/afternoon/evening, welcome to the Westin Austin at The Domain."
  • Help guest with chair, open napkin, welcome to event.
  • Introduce yourself and your partner once the table is seated. Describe menu and invite special requests.
  • Thank guests as they leave and wish them a good day/evening.
Decorum on the Floor
  • Work quietly. Don't call out to other associates.
  • Do not speak to a customer in another language unless they initiate it.
  • Do not chew gum or eat while on duty.
  • The guest ALWAYS has the right of way.
  • Food complaint? Remove and replace immediately. Inform the captain.
  • Accidents involving guests: offer soda water and a clean napkin first, then notify the captain.
🥂
Passing Hors d'Oeuvres
▶
  • Carry plate, platter, or tray in white-gloved hands. Offer a bev-nap to each guest.
  • Food must be one-to-two bite size. No toothpicks, frill picks, bones, or stems.
  • Servers must be able to name every item and its ingredients.
  • Sauces generally should not be offered alongside passed items to keep service clean.
BEO

How to Read a BEO
▶

Banquet Event Order — lists all information needed to carry out a function (food, beverage, room arrangements). Distributed to the host/meeting planner and all involved hotel staff.

A — Day/DateAlways double-check date and day match.
B — Group NameMay differ from posting info.
C — Reader BoardName as it appears on daily/weekly event sheets.
D — BEO File #Quick ID of the BEO.
G — ContactIndividual in charge of the group.
K — GuaranteeGuests confirmed within 72 hours. Minimum charge number.
L — SetCovers to prepare — includes overset above guarantee.
M — TimeMust specify start AND end time.
O — LocationWhere the event takes place. Note outdoor functions and backup rooms.
P — MenuAll food and beverage items for the function.
📢
Foyer Briefing (Pre-Function)
▶

Conducted by the Captain or Manager 1–2 hours before every banquet function. Purpose: review the BEO and all service details.

  • Uniform inspection: shoes, socks, nametag, pen & pad, wine opener — and a smile.
  • Table set review: linen color, napkin fold, china/glass/flatware placement.
  • Station and side work assignments. Mise en place location.
  • Menu and BEO walkthrough — ingredients, courses, wine pairings.
  • Bar hours, type (host or cash), and cleanup.
  • Special meals: vegetarian, kosher, children's, etc.
  • VIP assignments, buffet specialty stations, signals for service.

Wine Service Standards
▶

The parenthesis rule: Serve wine → Serve food → Clear food → Clear wine.

  • Pour from guest's right side. Label faces up toward guest. Cradle bottle from back — not with hand over top.
  • Finish with a slight clockwise twist to stop dripping.
  • Portions: white ½ full, red ½ full.
  • Max 4 glasses per place setting. Water glass at tip of knives; wine at 45° below.
  • Open bottles in the back aisle. Leave corks out when carrying into the room.
  • Never lift a wine glass from the table to pour.
  • If guest declines wine, remove the glass. Never flip it over.
  • Max 2 bottles per server at one time. No trays or napkin wrapping.
  • Host tasting not required unless specified on the BEO.

Wine 101 — Terminology
▶
AromaThe smell of wine. The nose can detect 350–800 scents.
BodyPerceived weight/texture. Skim milk = light; 2% = medium; cream = full.
DryNot sweet.
FinishThe impression the wine leaves as you swallow — does it linger or fade?
Sweet vs FruityFruity = aroma of fruit. Sweet = sugar on your tongue. Hold your nose to isolate sweetness.
TanninsFrom grape skins/seeds/stems. Dry, puckery sensation — like over-steeped tea.
FermentationHow alcohol develops: Sugar + Yeast = Alcohol + CO₂.
